# Questions for Sergei

Pick 4. Ask the role probe early (see `interview_brief.md`), keep two for the end, and drop the rest if time runs short.

Ordered by what they buy you.

---

## Ask early — you need the answer to aim your stories

1. **"Can you give me shape on the team? Is this closer to applied AI on product surfaces, AI platform and infrastructure, or trust and safety? And would I be managing engineers directly or managers?"**

2. **"Is this a new team, a backfill, or a team that's growing? What's the headcount picture over the next year?"**
   Tells you whether they want a builder or a stabilizer — and you're a builder, so if it's greenfield, say so.

---

## Strong middle questions

3. **"What does the first six months look like for whoever takes this? What would make you say it went well?"**
   The best single question in any manager interview. His answer is the actual job description.

4. **"Where does this team sit between research and product? Who decides what gets built?"**
   Gets at whether you'd own technical direction or execute someone else's roadmap. You want the former.

5. **"How do engineering and product split ownership on this team? Who owns the roadmap?"**
   Signals you take the Product partnership seriously — which is one of the four things he's assessing.

6. **"What's the hardest unsolved problem on the team right now?"**
   Then actually engage with the answer. This is where you get to be technical without it being a technical screen.

7. **"How technical do you want this manager to be? Some orgs want a manager who's in design reviews and reading code; some want one who's fully on people and strategy."**
   Directly relevant to how you operate, and lets you position the tech-lead-manager thing as a fit rather than an oddity.

---

## Good closers

8. **"What's changed most about how your org works in the last year?"**
   Big-company version of "what's the trajectory." Reveals reorgs, strategy shifts, and pressure.

9. **"What do the managers who do well on your team have in common?"**
   Tells you his actual bar, and it's a graceful way to ask "what are you looking for."

10. **"What's something about working here that surprised you?"**
    Humanizes the last two minutes and often gets you something honest.

---

## Don't ask tomorrow

- Compensation, leveling, or title — that's a recruiter conversation, and asking a hiring manager on a first screen reads as focused on the wrong thing.
- Anything answerable from LinkedIn's public site or press. It reads as not having prepared.
- "Is there room for growth?" — too generic. Question 9 gets you the same information and sounds better.
- Remote/hybrid policy — save it for the recruiter unless he raises it.

---

## If he asks "anything else you want me to know?"

Have one sentence ready. Suggested:

> "Just that I've built a team from zero once already, and that's the part of the job I actually like — not just running one that exists. If this team is in a building phase, that's the version of the role I'd be strongest in."
