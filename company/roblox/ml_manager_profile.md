# My ML Manager & Tech Lead Manager Background

## Current Role - Tech Lead Manager at DoorDash

So right now I'm working at DoorDash on the Risk ML team, which is focused on fraud detection. I'm leading about 10 ML engineers, and I'm basically wearing both hats - I'm the tech lead and the manager. 

What happened is our previous manager left the team, and I stepped up to take on both roles. It's been about a year now managing this team, and honestly it's been a great experience because I get to stay hands-on with the technical side while also building and developing the people on my team.

DoorDash has this interesting policy where because we're so AI-focused, managers can manage pretty large teams - 20+ people - so a team of 10 is pretty reasonable scope. I own both the technical strategy and roadmap for our fraud ML systems, as well as hiring, mentoring, and growing the team members.

The role involves a lot of cross-functional work too - I'm constantly aligning with engineering, operations, the business side, and even legal and compliance because fraud detection touches all of those areas. There's a lot of ambiguity and constantly evolving threats, so it's a high-stakes environment where you have to make smart tradeoffs between things like false positives and false negatives.

## Previous Experience - ML Manager at VoltronData

Before DoorDash, I spent almost two years as an ML Manager at VoltronData. That's where I really got my feet wet with leading ML teams - building teams, technical strategy, the whole thing. It gave me a solid foundation for what I'm doing now.

## The Arc

I think the way I'd describe my journey is that I've always been technically strong in ML - that's my foundation - but I've also learned how to lead people and teams effectively. Going from being an individual contributor or tech lead to stepping into a dual tech lead and manager role shows that I can wear multiple hats and step up when needed.

What I think makes me a good fit for critical harm roles is that I've been operating in high-stakes, safety-critical domains. Fraud detection is all about trust and safety for users and the platform. The problems are hard, the stakes are real, and you have to think deeply about tradeoffs and impact. I think that experience translates really well to critical harm work.
