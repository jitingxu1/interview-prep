# Q2. What do you value when assessing whether someone is a good fit for your team?

[← Round 1 index](README.md)

Your framework: **the bar moves with the level, and the weighting moves with what the team is currently missing.** That is a strong answer because it treats hiring as portfolio construction rather than ranking candidates on one axis.

**The script:**

> It depends on two things: the level I'm hiring for, and what my team is missing right now.
>
> For a **senior** hire, I weight judgment and independence most. Can they take an ambiguous problem, break it down, and make reasonable tradeoffs without me in the room? I also look for a multiplier effect — will they raise the level of the people around them through design reviews, code review, and mentoring? A senior engineer who only produces their own output is a miss at that level.
>
> For a **junior** hire, I'm betting on trajectory, not current output. Learning speed, coachability, strong fundamentals. Do they ask good questions? When they're shown they're wrong, how fast do they update? I'd take a fast learner with solid foundations over someone who simply knows more today.
>
> Then there's **team composition**. Hiring isn't only about the individual — it's about the gap. If my team is strong technically but weak at cross-team communication, I weight that heavily. If I have plenty of model builders and nobody who enjoys the infrastructure work, that's the gap I hire against. A great candidate who duplicates what we already have is a worse hire than a good candidate who fills the hole.

**How they grade it:**

| Signal | Strong | Red flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level awareness | Different bar for senior and junior, and says why | One bar for every level |
| Team-gap thinking | Hires against what the team lacks | Ranks candidates on raw strength only |
| Testability | Each value has a question that tests it (see [the questions I ask](interviewer_questions.md)) | "Culture fit," "smart and passionate" |
| Range | Wants people who will disagree with them | Describes hiring people like themselves |

**The follow-up that is coming:** "Tell me about a specific hire where the team-needs lens changed your decision — someone you hired or rejected mainly because of what the team was missing."

Two things to avoid in this answer: the phrase "culture fit" (the next question is always "what does that mean, concretely?"), and a list of virtues with no mechanism for testing them. [The questions I ask](interviewer_questions.md) is that mechanism.
