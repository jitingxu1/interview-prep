# Q6. How do you build credibility with reports on a team you didn't build?

[← Round 1 index](README.md)

This question is really about Stripe itself — if they hire you, this is your first ninety days. Answer it as though you are describing what you would do there, not only what you did somewhere else.

**The script:**

> I assume on day one that I have no credibility and that the team is reasonably skeptical — a new manager is a risk to them, not an upgrade. So I don't try to prove myself by having opinions early.
>
> First few weeks I'm mostly listening. In 1:1s I ask three things: what's working that I should not touch, what's broken that you've given up on, and what do you want in two years. The second question is the highest-value one — the thing everyone has complained about and nobody fixed.
>
> Then I pick one of those and fix it fast. Something visible and unglamorous — a flaky test suite, an on-call rotation nobody rebalanced, a review process that's a bottleneck, an approval I can absorb myself. Credibility comes from removing a specific pain, not from a vision document.
>
> In parallel I go deep enough technically to be useful. I don't need to be the best engineer on the team, but I need to understand the system well enough that they don't have to simplify things for me, and well enough that I can tell when an estimate is off.
>
> And I'm explicit about how I work — how I make decisions, what I'll escalate, what I expect them to decide without me. Most of the anxiety with a new manager is uncertainty about the rules, so I state the rules.

**What earns extra points:** mentioning that you protect what the previous manager built. Teams watch for whether a new manager needs to rewrite everything to feel ownership. Saying "the first thing I looked for was what was already working, and I said so publicly" is a mature, memorable note.

**How they grade it:**

| Signal | Strong | Red flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listening first | Structured early 1:1s with specific questions | Arrives with a reorg plan |
| Early win | Fixes one concrete, unglamorous pain fast | A vision document as the first deliverable |
| Respect for the past | Names what was working and keeps it | Rewrites things to feel ownership |
| Technical depth | Learns the system well enough to judge an estimate | Rubber-stamps engineers, or overrides them |
| Measurement | Knows how they would tell it is working at 90 days | No success signal at all |

**Follow-ups to prepare:**

- "What if the team was loyal to the previous manager and resented the change?"
- "What would you do if, in those first weeks, you found something you thought was badly broken — would you still wait?"
- "How would you know, after ninety days, whether it was working?" (Have an answer: people bringing you problems before they become crises, disagreeing with you in meetings, and 1:1s that stop being status reports.)
