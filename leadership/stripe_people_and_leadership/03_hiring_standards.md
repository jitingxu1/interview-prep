# Q3. What are your standards for hiring, and how do you gauge whether someone meets them?

[← Round 1 index](README.md)

Q2 asked what you value. This one asks how high the bar is and how you verify it. The scoring point is at the end: **does your bar have teeth?**

**The script:**

> I start by writing down what the role has to deliver in its first year — not a list of technologies, but outcomes. That becomes the scorecard, and it's what I calibrate the loop against. If a signal isn't on the scorecard, I don't test for it.
>
> Then I design the loop so each signal has an owner. One interview goes deep on a project they actually shipped — that's where I test judgment and whether they really operated at the scope on their resume. One is hands-on technical. One is collaboration and how they handle disagreement. Everyone writes up their assessment independently before we debrief, so the loudest voice in the room doesn't anchor everyone else.
>
> I look for two signals in particular. First: have they genuinely operated at this scope before — not in theory, but with the messy parts? Second, the slope question — is their trajectory steep enough that they'll be operating a level above within a year?
>
> And I hold the bar when it's expensive. [YOUR EXAMPLE — see below.]

**The closing example is the whole answer.** A bar discussed without a cost is a bar on paper. The shape you want: a role stayed open while the team was stretched thin, the finalists were "good but not above the bar," and you said no anyway — then what that decision cost, and what happened when you eventually hired the right person.

Fill in: how long the role stayed open, who was pressuring you to just hire someone, what you said to them, and how the eventual hire worked out. If you have ever reversed the other way — lowered the bar and regretted it — that version also works, and self-aware beats flawless.

**How they grade it:**

| Signal | Strong | Red flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bar definition | Written scorecard tied to first-year outcomes | "I know it when I see it" |
| Process integrity | Independent write-ups before the debrief | Group debrief where the most senior voice decides |
| Teeth | A role held open at real, named cost | Has never said no under hiring pressure |
| Learning | A mis-hire and the process change that followed | Claims no mis-hires |

**Follow-ups to prepare:**

- "How do you know your bar isn't just a preference for people like you?" (Answer with the scorecard and independent write-ups — mechanisms, not intentions.)
- "Tell me about a hire you got wrong. What did you miss, and what did you change in your process afterward?"
- "How do you calibrate a new interviewer on your team?"
