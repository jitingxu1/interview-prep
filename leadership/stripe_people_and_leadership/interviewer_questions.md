# The questions I ask, and what I listen for

[← Round 1 index](README.md)

This is the proof layer for Q2 and Q3. If you can name the question you ask and the signal you grade on, your bar stops being an adjective.

| Dimension | Question I ask | Strong signal | Red flag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Judgment (senior) | "Tell me about the most ambiguous project you owned end to end. When nobody could tell you what to do, how did you decide?" | Defines the problem themselves; names tradeoffs | Waits to be told what to build |
| Conviction (senior) | "Walk me through a technical decision others disagreed with. How did you defend it, and what happened?" | Explains why A over B; can describe losing well | Only tells stories where they were proven right |
| Multiplier (senior) | "Who have you grown, and what did you actually do?" | Names people and specific interventions | Describes own output only |
| Learning speed (junior) | "Tell me about something technical you learned recently. Walk me through how you learned it." | A systematic path; knows what they skipped | Scattered, tutorial-deep only |
| Coachability | "What's the most useful critical feedback you've received, and what did you change?" | Specific — who, when, what changed after | "I take feedback well" with no example |
| Updating | "What's something you changed your mind about recently?" | Names the evidence that moved them | Cannot produce an example |
| Collaboration | "Tell me about working with a team whose priorities conflicted with yours." | Found a shared goal | Blames the other team throughout |
| Glue work | "Tell me about work nobody wanted to do that you picked up." | Took it because it unblocked others | Only chose visible, high-credit work |

**The live test beats all of them.** Mid-answer, challenge their position once: *"Interesting — but wouldn't X be a problem with that approach?"* Stories can be rehearsed; the reaction cannot. Pausing, thinking, "that's fair, let me reconsider" — then either adjusting or defending with reasons — is the signal. Repeating the original answer louder, or dismissing the premise, is the red flag.

Also worth saying out loud in the round: you append **"what would you do differently?"** to every behavioral question you ask. It is the same follow-up Stripe will use on you, and naming it shows you run interviews the way they do.

One reference-check line that is worth having ready: asking a former manager *"what kind of environment does this person need to do their best work?"* — it surfaces real failure modes without asking anyone to badmouth a colleague.
