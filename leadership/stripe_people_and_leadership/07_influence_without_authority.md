# Q7. Tell me about a time you influenced without formal authority

[← Round 1 index](README.md)

For a manager at Stripe this is close to the whole job — most of what you need will be owned by someone who does not report to you. Your foundation-versus-firefighting story (Round 2, Q13) is partly an influence story; if you use it there, bring a different one here.

**The frame:**

1. **What I needed and did not control.** A platform team's roadmap slot, another team's data, a standard adopted across groups, a decision owned by a peer.
2. **Why they had no reason to say yes.** Be honest about their incentives. Influence stories are only interesting when the other side was rationally resistant.
3. **What I changed about the ask.** The move is usually one of: reframe it as their win, make it cheap for them, bring evidence they can't argue with, or find the one person whose opinion the others follow and convince them first.
4. **What I actually said.** Have one or two real sentences ready. This is where "what exactly did you say?" gets asked.
5. **The outcome, and the relationship afterward.** Whether you could go back to that team a second time is the real test of how you won.

**Strong versions of this story:** getting another team to adopt your shared pipeline or standard instead of building their own; convincing a partner team to take on work that benefited you more than them; changing a decision already made above you by bringing data rather than escalating.

**Weak versions to avoid:** stories where you won by escalating to a shared manager (that is authority, borrowed), and stories where the other side was simply wrong and you corrected them (that is being right, not influencing).

**How they grade it:**

| Signal | Strong | Red flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empathy for incentives | Explains why the other side was rationally resistant | Other side painted as wrong or obstructive |
| Changed the ask | Reframed it, made it cheap, or brought evidence | Repeated the same ask, louder |
| Escalation discipline | Considered escalating and chose not to, with a reason | Won through a shared manager |
| The words | Has the actual sentence they said | Paraphrase only |
| Relationship | Could go back to that team for a second ask | Won once and burned the bridge |

**Follow-ups to prepare:**

- "What if they had still said no?"
- "Did you consider escalating? Why didn't you?"
- "How did you find out what mattered to them?"
