# Q8. Tell me about a conflict you resolved

[← Round 1 index](README.md)

Prepare two: one **engineer-to-engineer** on your team, and one **cross-team** with a peer manager. Interviewers often ask for whichever you did not volunteer.

**Engineer-to-engineer — the frame:**

> Two strong engineers, a real technical disagreement that had turned personal. What I try to establish first is whether this is a disagreement about facts, about values, or about respect — because only the first one can be settled with evidence.
>
> I talk to each separately first, to understand what each actually wants rather than what they're arguing about. Then, if it's a technical question, I make us write down what we'd have to believe for each option to be right, and what evidence would settle it. That converts a fight into a question.
>
> If it isn't resolvable that way, I decide, I explain the reasoning to both of them, and I say explicitly that I'm not declaring either of them wrong. An unresolved technical argument costs more than a decision one person dislikes.

**The judgment note worth saying out loud:** not all conflict should be resolved. Healthy disagreement about approach is how the team gets to a better answer, and a manager who steps in too early trains people to escalate instead of working it out. You intervene when it starts costing velocity, when it becomes personal, or when it starts to spill onto other people.

**Cross-team — the frame:** two teams with conflicting priorities, both locally correct. Establish the shared goal one level up, make the tradeoff explicit in writing, and if it still doesn't resolve, escalate *jointly* with the peer manager rather than separately — arriving together with a framed decision is materially different from each side lobbying.

**Fill in:** your actual two stories, and in particular what you said in the first one-on-one conversation.

**How they grade it:**

| Signal | Strong | Red flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diagnosis | Separates facts, values, and respect | Treats every conflict as a technical question |
| Fairness | Hears each side separately first | Sides with the more senior or louder person |
| Decisiveness | Decides when it is stuck and explains the reasoning | Lets it fester to keep the peace |
| Timing | Knows when not to intervene | Steps into every disagreement — or none |
| Cross-team | Escalates jointly with the peer manager | Lobbies their own leadership separately |
| Honesty | Owns what it cost, including attrition | Everyone-was-happy ending |

**Follow-ups to prepare:**

- "Did either of them leave afterward?" (Answer honestly if yes — and say what you learned.)
- "What would you have done if your decision turned out to be the wrong technical call?"
- "How long did you let it run before stepping in, and was that right?"
