# Q5. What is your approach to managing poor performers?

[← Round 1 index](README.md)

The trap in this question is sounding either soft ("I coach everyone, it always works out") or cold ("I move fast on managing people out"). What scores is a **process** with a clear diagnosis step and a willingness to reach a conclusion either way.

**The script:**

> My first step is diagnosis, because "underperforming" is a symptom with very different causes. Is it a skill gap, a motivation problem, a role mismatch, something happening outside work, or — honestly — is it me, because I set unclear expectations? Each of those has a different fix, and acting before I know which one I'm looking at wastes everyone's time.
>
> Then I make the gap explicit and written. Here is what the level requires, here is what I'm observing, here is the specific difference. People are usually not surprised by the content — they are surprised by the severity, which means I waited too long. So I say the hard sentence early and plainly.
>
> Then we agree on a plan with observable outcomes and a real timeline — not "improve communication," but "lead the design review for X and I'll give you feedback after each one." I check in weekly, and I write down what happens.
>
> And I reach a conclusion. If it's a role mismatch, sometimes the best outcome is a different team where their strengths fit — I've found that lands better than people expect. If it isn't improving, I move to exit, and I do it without drama and with as much dignity as I can preserve. Dragging it out is the cruelest option: it's unfair to them, and the rest of the team already knows.

**Add your own example.** The best version shows two outcomes if you have them: one person who turned it around (what specifically changed), and one who did not (how you handled the exit, and what you told the team afterward).

**How they grade it:**

| Signal | Strong | Red flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diagnosis | Separates skill, will, fit, context — and themselves — before acting | Same playbook for every case |
| Directness | Says the hard sentence early, in writing | Hints for months; the person is surprised by the severity |
| Plan quality | Observable outcomes and a real timeline | "Improve communication" |
| Closure | Reaches a conclusion either way, with dignity | Lets it drift — or jumps straight to a PIP |
| Team awareness | Considers what the team saw and what they were told | Treats it as purely a 1:1 matter |

**Follow-ups to prepare:**

- "What exactly did you say in the first conversation?" — they want your actual words. Rehearse one opening line out loud.
- "How long did you wait before that conversation, and was that too long?"
- "How did the rest of the team react?"
- "What did you do differently with the next person who started to slip?" (The mechanism answer: earlier expectation-setting, clearer level definitions, no-surprises reviews.)
- "Have you ever been wrong about someone being a poor performer?"
