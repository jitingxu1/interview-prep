## How this round is scored

This round measures the organizational complexity you have actually operated in — not how polished the story sounds. Interviewers push several layers deep on one story rather than collecting many.

Use this shape for every story: **Situation → Constraints → Decision → Tradeoff → Outcome → Reflection**.

The four follow-ups that come up most, per candidate reports:

- "Why didn't you choose the other approach?"
- "What exactly did you say to them?" (they want the words, not a paraphrase)
- "What tradeoff were you optimizing for?"
- "What would you do differently now?"

Each question file ends with a **How they grade it** table — the same strong-signal / red-flag rubric you use on candidates, turned around on yourself. Read the red-flag column before rehearsing; it is what the follow-ups are fishing for.

Three failure modes to avoid:

1. **"We" instead of "I."** The most common senior-candidate miss: the interviewer cannot tell what you personally decided. Name your decision, then the team's execution.
2. **No alternative named.** A decision with no rejected option reads as a default, not a judgment call.
3. **No number.** Quantify the outcome, or say plainly that you could not measure it and why.

One thing to prepare that is not a story: a question of your own about team health and attrition. Several 2026 forum posts describe elevated performance-management activity at Stripe this year. Treat anonymous chatter with salt, but asking about it is reasonable and signals you evaluate teams the way you would want to be evaluated.
