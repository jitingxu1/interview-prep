# "How do you build trust with a team, especially one that didn't choose you?"

Framework, spoken answer, traps, and follow-ups. Built Nov 24. Company-specific version: [Rula / Jay Floyd Q14](../company/rula/jay-floyd-leadership-qa.md). Related: [Stripe prep: credibility with an inherited team](./stripe_people_and_leadership/06_credibility_inherited_team.md), [building_team_and_culture.md](./building_team_and_culture.md) (Part B, inheriting a team).

**Your real material:** you inherited a team of 10 engineers after their manager left (on the verified list).

---

## What the interviewer is testing

1. You **listen before you change things**.
2. You **earn trust through actions** (keeping promises), not through your title.
3. You show the team **you're on their side**.

---

## Framework: listen → one visible win → keep every promise → no surprises → on their side

| Step | What it means | ~Time |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Listen before changing anything** | 1:1s with everyone: what's working, what was missing, where they want to go | 20s |
| **2. One visible win, fast** | Fix a small, real pain they named, within weeks | 15s |
| **3. Keep every promise** | Start small and never miss. Trust is built on consistency | 15s |
| **4. No surprises** | Expectations in writing, feedback early, share context openly | 10s |
| **5. Be on their side** | Absorb org noise, give credit publicly, take the hits | 10s |
| **6. Result** | What changed | 10s |

---

## Final answer (~90 seconds)

> I've lived this. I inherited a team of ten engineers when their manager left, with no interim manager, so they didn't choose me and didn't know me.
>
> **Listen first.** I didn't change anything for the first few weeks. In my first 1:1s, the questions that mattered weren't "what are you working on?" but "what was missing with your last manager?" and "where do you actually want to go?" You're learning what the team believes about how things work here before you try to change it.
>
> **One visible win.** People judge a new manager by whether the first promise survives pressure. So I picked something small and real that they'd asked for: consistent 1:1s and protected focus time. And I never missed it, even in busy weeks.
>
> **No surprises.** I wrote down expectations, gave feedback early, and shared context openly: what leadership was asking for and why.
>
> **On their side.** I took the stakeholder noise and the meetings myself, so they didn't have to. When people see you absorb the pressure instead of passing it down, they start to trust you've got them.
>
> **[REAL RESULT: e.g. "Within a couple of months, people started bringing me problems early instead of hiding them. Nobody left in the first year." Only what's true.]**
>
> Trust isn't built by being loud. It's built by being steady and predictable when it counts.

**[CONFIRM BEFORE USING LIVE: was there really no interim manager? What did people actually ask for in those first 1:1s? A real result (retention, people raising problems early, delivery, engagement). Consistency: if the inherited team is the same team as your pods or areas, keep the numbers consistent across answers.]**

**Why this lands:** for Rula's Jay Floyd, this is "calm authority": steady, predictable, trust built before it's needed.

---

## Traps

- **Big changes in week one.** Tells the team their way was wrong.
- **Criticising the previous manager.** The team gets defensive and wonders what you'll say about them.
- **Proving yourself technically** by winning arguments.
- **Promises you can't keep.** One broken promise early costs more than ten kept ones earn.

---

## Quick hint

> **Listen first → one visible win → never miss a promise → no surprises → absorb the noise.**
> Key line: *"People judge a new manager by whether the first promise survives pressure."*

---

## Likely follow-ups

| Follow-up | Hint | Key line |
|---|---|---|
| What if someone on the team wanted your job? | **Name it privately, give them real scope, let work convince** | "I don't pull rank. I let my work do the convincing over time." |
| The team was loyal to the old manager? | **Respect what worked, keep it, change slowly** | "Inheriting a team is mostly subtraction, not addition." |
| What would you do in your first 90 days here? | **Listen (30) → one visible win (60) → first real change (90)** | "Understand what's working before I change anything." |
| How do you know you've earned trust? | **People bring bad news early; they disagree with you openly** | "When people tell me problems before they're problems." |

The first follow-up is close to "leading former peers." If it fits a real situation, this answer can double as that one.
