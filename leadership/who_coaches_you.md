# "Who coaches you? How do you stay coachable as a manager?"

Framework, draft answer, traps, and follow-ups. Built Nov 24. Company-specific version: [Rula / Jay Floyd Q21](../company/rula/jay-floyd-leadership-qa.md).

---

## What the interviewer is testing

1. You actually **have** sources of coaching, not just "I read books."
2. You **ask** for feedback, especially from your own team.
3. You **change** because of it, with a real example.

For a coaching-first interviewer like Rula's Jay Floyd ("even the best coaches have their own coaches"), this question matters more than it looks.

---

## Framework: who → how → example → close

| Step | What to say | ~Time |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Who** | Two or three real sources: your manager, a mentor or peer managers, your HR partner, **your own team** | 20s |
| **2. How** | Your habits: asking specific questions, the reflection loop, acting on feedback visibly | 20s |
| **3. Example** | One piece of feedback you got and what you changed | 25s |
| **4. Close** | Why it matters for a manager | 10s |

**Your real material:**
- **HR partner:** you practised difficult conversations with her before having them.
- **Your reports:** in 1:1s, you ask what's working and what isn't in how you lead.
- **The reflection loop** (from the weakness practice in [weaknesses.md](./weaknesses.md)): after a hard situation, write down what happened, how you handled it, what you'd do better, what in the system allowed it, and which instinct got in the way. Then go over it with a mentor, and sometimes use AI as a sparring partner.
- **Books and podcasts:** mention once at most. It's one-way learning.

## Draft answer (~75 seconds)

> I have a few sources, and the most important one is my own team.
>
> **Who:** I have a mentor, a more experienced manager I go to for the hard people situations. Our HR partner has coached me too: before my first few difficult conversations, I practised them with her. And I ask my reports directly in 1:1s: "What's one thing I could do differently that would help you?" If I'm asking them to be coachable, I have to model it.
>
> **How:** after any hard situation, I write down what happened, how I handled it, what I'd do better, and which instinct got in my way. Then I bring the pattern to my mentor. That's how reading and advice turn into actual habits.
>
> **Example:** the clearest one was feedback. I used to soften hard messages and wait for a better moment. My HR partner helped me see that waiting was the problem, not the message. I changed to a simple structure (what I saw, why it matters, what good looks like, how I'll help), and I give feedback within a week. Nobody on my team gets surprised at review time anymore.
>
> **Close:** the day I stop being coached is the day I start assuming my instincts are right, and my instincts are exactly what I've had to rebuild as a manager.

**[CONFIRM BEFORE USING LIVE: do you actually have a mentor? If not, say "peer managers" or "my manager" instead. Don't claim a mentor you don't have.]**

**Stronger example, if you have one:** feedback **from one of your own reports**, e.g. *"One engineer told me my design-review comments felt like I was making the decision for them. Now I ask questions first."* It shows your team feels safe telling you hard things, which links to the culture answer in [building_team_and_culture.md](./building_team_and_culture.md). **[NEEDS YOUR STORY, if you have one.]**

## Traps

- **"I read a lot of books and listen to podcasts" as the main answer.** One-way learning, not coaching.
- **Saying you're studying the interviewer's material.** It reads as flattery.
- **No example of changing.** Being coachable means acting on feedback, not just hearing it.
- **AI as your main coach.** Fine as one phrase, but lead with people.

---

## Likely follow-ups

| Follow-up | Hint | Key line |
|---|---|---|
| Hardest feedback you've received? | **The feedback story: waiting was the problem** | "The discomfort of giving feedback early is cheaper than the damage of giving it late." |
| How do you get honest feedback from your reports? | **Specific questions, thank them, act visibly** | "Not 'any feedback?' but 'what's one thing I could do differently?'" |
| What are you working on right now as a leader? | **The answer-man instinct, in one line** | "Asking 'what do you think?' before I answer." |

**Quick hint:** *Who (mentor, HR partner, my team) → how (ask specifically, reflection loop) → example (feedback → change) → close.* Key line: *"If I'm asking them to be coachable, I have to model it."*
