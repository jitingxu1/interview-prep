# Managing Up — Working Effectively With Your Manager

Managing up means actively running your working relationship with your manager so they can better support you, your team, and the business. It isn't flattery. It's treating your manager as your most important stakeholder.

Related: [`reporting_up.md`](./reporting_up.md) (how to brief leadership), [`weaknesses.md`](./weaknesses.md#2-too-technical-when-reporting-up) (weakness #2), [`stripe_strategy_and_execution/10a_saying_no_to_senior.md`](./stripe_strategy_and_execution/10a_saying_no_to_senior.md) (saying no upward).

---

## Watch the word: ByteDance means something different

ByteStyle's **Be Candid and Clear** explicitly says "Avoid 'managing up'." There it's **negative**:
- Reporting only good news
- Guessing what the boss wants to hear and saying it
- Spending effort on keeping leadership happy instead of solving problems

**In ByteDance / TikTok interviews, don't use the phrase "managing up" for yourself.** Describe the behavior instead: "I keep my manager informed early," "I push back with data." At most US companies (Google, Meta, Stripe), "managing up" is positive and fine to use.

---

## Six things to do

### 1. Know your manager's goals and pressures
- What are their OKRs? What does *their* manager care about most? What are they most worried about this quarter?
- **Connect your team's work to their goals.** "This gets you to your Q4 X target" beats "we're working on X."
- Ask directly: "What are you most worried about this quarter?" / "What would make this a great half for you?"

### 2. No surprises
- **Bad news early, with a plan.** Template: [`reporting_up.md`](./reporting_up.md#3-bad-news).
- What a manager hates most is hearing about your team's problem from someone else first.
- If you're going to their manager or another VP, **tell your manager first.**

### 3. Learn how they like to communicate
- Written or verbal? Detail or just conclusions? Weekly is enough, or do they want real-time?
- Ask in the first 1:1: "How do you like to get updates? What do you want to hear about immediately vs. weekly?"
- Then **do it their way**, not your default.

### 4. Own the 1:1
- Send the agenda ahead. Use the time for **things you need them for**: decisions, resources, alignment they can unblock.
- Don't read out status — put that in a doc.
- Ask regularly: "Is there anything I should be doing differently?"

### 5. Disagree openly, then commit
- If you disagree, say so — with data and alternatives. Once, clearly, in writing.
- If they still want the original call, **commit fully.** No relitigating, no half-hearted execution.
- Playbook: [`10a_saying_no_to_senior.md`](./stripe_strategy_and_execution/10a_saying_no_to_senior.md) — ask what it needs to achieve, offer options, make the cost concrete.

### 6. Make it easy for them to make you visible
- Give them your team's wins in a form they can pass up: "Here's a one-liner you can share upward."
- Ask for what they're there for: resources, air cover, unblocking a cross-team issue.
- Tell them your career goal: "I want to grow toward managing managers. What would you need to see from me?"

## How this connects

- **Weakness #2 (too technical when reporting up):** Points 1 and 3 are the fix — know what your manager cares about, then deliver it the way they take it in.
- **TikTok:** Your manager and skip (Head of PGC) may sit in China or work closely with HQ. Across time zones, **no surprises** and **written async updates** matter even more — you can't count on a meeting whenever you need one.
- **Likely interview questions:** "How do you work with your manager?" / "Tell me about a time you disagreed with your boss." Answer with points 2 and 5. Skip the phrase "managing up" at ByteDance.

## 30-second spoken version

> "I treat my manager as my most important stakeholder. I make sure I understand what they're measured on and connect my team's work to it. I never let them be surprised — bad news goes to them first, early, with a plan. When I disagree, I say so once, clearly, with data, and if the call stands, I commit fully. And I make it easy for them to represent my team upward — I give them the one-line version of our wins."
