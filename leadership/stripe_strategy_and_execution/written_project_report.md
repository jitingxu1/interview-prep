# The written project report — start it now

[← Round 2 index](README.md)

Candidates report that after the early rounds Stripe asks EM candidates to prepare a written report on a project they led as a manager, then walk through it interactively later in the loop. This is from candidate write-ups, not from Stripe directly — worth confirming with your recruiter, but cheap to prepare either way, because writing it is also the best possible prep for this round.

**Pick the project on these criteria:** you were the manager, not the engineer; it had real ambiguity; it involved other teams; and you can talk about what went wrong. A flawless project is a weak choice — there is nothing for the interviewer to dig into, and the dig is the interview.

**Outline to draft:**

1. **Context and goal.** Why this mattered, and what was uncertain when you started.
2. **How I planned it.** Milestones, the dependencies you identified, and the risks you named up front — including one you got wrong.
3. **The team and the roles.** Who did what, and what you decided versus delegated.
4. **The tracking mechanism.** How you knew where things stood without asking for status — be specific; this is the part most reports skip.
5. **What slipped, and what I did.** The most valuable section. What the early signal was, how long before you acted, and what you cut or renegotiated.
6. **Outcome.** Metrics, including what you missed.
7. **What I'd do differently.** Two or three real items.

**How they grade it:**

| Signal | Strong | Red flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Project choice | Real ambiguity, cross-team, and something went wrong | A flawless project with nothing to dig into |
| Manager altitude | What they decided versus delegated | Reads like an engineer's design doc |
| Tracking mechanism | How they knew status without asking for it | Skipped, or "weekly syncs" |
| Slippage handling | The early signal, how long before acting, what was cut | Nothing slipped |
| Honest outcome | Metrics including misses | Only the wins |
| Brevity | Two to three pages that invite questions | Long enough to pre-empt every question |

**Write it tight** — two to three pages. The report's job is to give the interviewer good places to dig, not to pre-empt the conversation. Every section should invite a "why that?"

One practical note: rehearse walking through it from the top in about five minutes, because you will likely be asked to summarize before they start probing.
