# Q12. How do you balance team priorities against organizational priorities?

[← Round 2 index](README.md)

The wrong answers are at both extremes: "the org's priorities win" (you're a relay, not a manager) and "I protect my team's focus" (you're a local optimizer). The answer they want sits in between and has a decision rule.

**The script:**

> My default assumption is that if they conflict, I probably don't understand the org's priority well enough yet — so the first move is to go find out what problem it's really solving. A surprising amount of apparent conflict is a translation problem.
>
> When the conflict is real, I separate two cases. If the org's priority is genuinely more important than what we're doing, I reprioritize, and I say so clearly — including to the people whose work just got shelved. What I don't do is accept the new work and quietly keep the old commitments alive, which is how teams end up at 130% and missing both.
>
> The other case is when I think the org's priority is a worse use of my team than what we're already doing. Then I owe my leadership an argument, not compliance and not resistance. I bring the cost in concrete terms — here's what gets delayed, here's who's affected, here's what I'd recommend instead — and I make the tradeoff visible so the decision gets made with real information. If the call still goes against me, I commit to it fully and I don't relitigate it with my team.
>
> That last part matters: once a decision is made above me, my team should hear it as my decision, not as something imposed on me. Disagreeing publicly after the fact is how managers lose their teams and their leadership at the same time.

**The line that lands:** "disagree, then commit — and the team hears it in my voice, not as a complaint about leadership."

**How they grade it:**

| Signal | Strong | Red flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curiosity first | Assumes missing context and goes to find it | Treats the org ask as an interruption |
| Decision rule | Separates "org is right" from "org is wrong" and acts differently | Always complies, or always protects the team |
| No hidden load | Drops old commitments explicitly when taking new ones | Takes it all on and runs at 130% |
| Argument, not resistance | Brings cost, impact, and a recommendation | Complains, or stalls quietly |
| Commit | Presents the final call to the team as their own | "Leadership made us do this" |

**Follow-ups to prepare:**

- "Tell me about a time you pushed back on an org priority. What happened?" (Your [foundation story](13_foundation_story.md) fits here too.)
- "And a time you lost that argument — how did you present it to your team?"
- "What would you have done if your team strongly disagreed with you accepting the change?"
