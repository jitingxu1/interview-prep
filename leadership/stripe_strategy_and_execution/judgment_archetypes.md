# Judgment archetypes to mine

[← Round 2 index](README.md)

You have one strong judgment story. These are the other shapes worth checking your history for — if one of them matches something you actually lived, it fills a gap in the story bank.

| Archetype | The judgment being tested | What makes it land |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The kill decision | Knowing when sunk cost is just sunk cost | How you handled the team's morale afterward |
| The unglamorous staffing call | Putting your strongest engineer on migration or reliability work instead of the interesting project | Naming the alternative you gave up |
| The hiring bet | Trusting loop signal over pedigree | The outcome a year later |
| The deadline pushback | When to absorb pressure versus pass it down | What you negotiated instead — scope cut, phased delivery |
| Build versus adopt | Team pride versus focus on the mission | Fits Stripe's practical-over-flashy bias well |
| The rollback on weak signal | Acting before the evidence was conclusive | Your escalation threshold, stated as a rule |

For any of them, the structure is identical: **the real alternatives on the table → what you knew and what you didn't → the tradeoff you optimized for → the outcome → what you'd change.** That five-part shape is what a judgment answer looks like, and it is the thing the "why not the other approach?" follow-up is probing for.

The archetype that most reliably impresses is the kill decision, because most managers cannot let go of work their team is invested in. If you have one, it belongs in this round.
