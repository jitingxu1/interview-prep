# Q15. What is the most impactful thing you've done, and what made it impactful?

[← Round 2 index](README.md)

The second half of the question is the one being scored. "What made it so impactful" is asking for your theory of impact — and a manager's answer should be about leverage, not about a thing you personally built.

**Pick on this basis:** the work where the effect outlived your involvement. A model you shipped is impact; a capability that let five teams ship their own models is leverage. Your foundation story qualifies on exactly this test — so if you use it for Q13, have a second option here, or tell this one from the leverage angle instead of the pushback angle.

**The structure:**

1. **What was true before, in one sentence.** The cost of the old state, quantified.
2. **What I decided, and what I turned down to do it.**
3. **What changed, with numbers.** Both the direct metric and the second-order effect — what other people could do afterward that they couldn't before.
4. **Why it mattered beyond my team.** This is the "what made it impactful" half. Answer in terms of the org, not the artifact.
5. **What it cost.** Impact stories with no cost read as marketing.

**A note on honesty that scores well here:** if the most impactful thing was not the most visible thing, say so. Managers who can distinguish "the work that got me promoted" from "the work that mattered most" come across as considerably more senior.

**How they grade it:**

| Signal | Strong | Red flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leverage | Impact that outlived their involvement | A thing they personally built |
| Theory of impact | Explains why it mattered to the org, not just the team | Describes the artifact only |
| Quantified | Direct metric plus a second-order effect | Adjectives instead of numbers |
| Attribution honesty | Separates their decision from the team's execution | "We" throughout, or "I" for everything |
| Cost | Names what it cost or displaced | Pure success story |
| Durability | Knows whether it survived them leaving | Never checked |

**Follow-ups to prepare:**

- "Would it have happened without you?" (A hard, good question. The honest answer is usually "eventually, but later and worse" — and then why.)
- "What was your actual contribution versus your team's?"
- "Is it still in use? Did it survive you leaving?"
- "What's the most impactful thing you *tried* that didn't work?"
