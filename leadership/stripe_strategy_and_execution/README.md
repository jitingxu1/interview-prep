## What this round tests

This round asks whether you can own complex delivery end to end: how your team's mission connects to the org's, how you plan, how you see risk early, and how you stay close to the details without taking over.

**The trap is the hero story.** The instinct is to narrate a project you personally saved. What scores is the opposite — **systems**: how you plan, track, detect slippage, and decide. A manager who rescued one launch is less interesting than one who built a way of working that makes rescues rare.

**The frame for any project walkthrough:** goal → plan and milestones → risks and dependencies → how you tracked progress → what slipped and what you did → outcome with metrics.

Three things this round rewards specifically:

1. **Metric reasoning.** Stripe probes metrics harder than most companies. Know your numbers cold, know which ones were proxies, and be ready to say which metric you would not trust again.
2. **Named tradeoffs.** Every plan gave something up. Say what, and who was unhappy about it.
3. **Depth without micromanagement.** Have a clear answer for when you dive into details yourself versus delegate — and an example of each. "Dive deep" does not mean reciting technical detail; it means knowing which metric matters and finding the one detail worth digging into.

Each question file ends with a **How they grade it** table — the strong-signal / red-flag rubric the interviewer is likely using. Read the red-flag column before rehearsing; it is what the follow-ups are fishing for.

Also expect the same deep follow-up pattern as round 1: *why not the other approach, what exactly did you say, what would you do differently.*
