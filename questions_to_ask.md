# Questions to Ask Roblox

These questions show you've done your homework, care about the role, and are thinking strategically.

---

## About the Role & Scope

### "Can you walk me through the team structure and what success looks like in Year 1?"
Why: You want to understand if it's a greenfield build, fixing a broken team, or scaling. Success metrics tell you what they care about.

**Listen for:**
- Are they building from scratch or inheriting a team?
- Do they care more about ML capability, team scaling, or safety metrics?
- Is there a board-level push or is this organic growth?

---

### "What are the biggest technical/organizational challenges the team is facing right now?"
Why: This tells you what you're actually walking into. If they say "we have no ML models," that's different from "we have models but can't scale them."

**Listen for:**
- Is it a technical problem (model performance) or organizational (team scaling)?
- Are they overwhelmed by scale or by complexity?
- Is there political/organizational friction?

---

### "How does the Critical Harm team interact with other parts of Trust & Safety?"
Why: You need to understand the org structure. Is Critical Harm isolated or integrated? Who else do you depend on?

**Listen for:**
- Are there turf wars or clear boundaries?
- Who owns what (moderation, policy, ML, etc.)?
- How healthy is the cross-functional relationship?

---

### "What does success look like for the team over the next 2-3 years? Is it growing headcount, improving model accuracy, building new systems?"
Why: Long-term vision tells you if this is a stable role or if you're constantly firefighting.

**Listen for:**
- Is there a 5-year plan or just 6-month OKRs?
- Are they trying to be a product team or a support function?
- Is there investment in the team or are you pulling from other orgs?

---

## About Managing

### "How do you think about manager-to-IC balance in this organization?"
Why: You want to understand if they expect you to code, and if they value technical leadership in managers.

**Listen for:**
- Do they have tech leads separate from managers?
- Do managers code?
- Is this a "move people around" role or a "build systems" role?

---

### "How much autonomy would I have in hiring and team composition?"
Why: You want to know if you can build the team you think is right, or if they're prescriptive.

**Listen for:**
- Can you grow the team?
- Do they have a hiring process or do you set it?
- Can you level/promote people quickly or is there HR bureaucracy?

---

### "What does career growth look like for engineers on this team?"
Why: You care about developing people. Understanding the company's growth trajectory helps you know if you can grow great engineers there.

**Listen for:**
- Are there paths to staff engineer, principal, etc.?
- Do senior engineers stay or leave?
- Is there mobility across teams?

---

### "Tell me about a manager who was really effective in this organization. What did they do well?"
Why: This tells you what success looks like in Roblox's culture.

**Listen for:**
- Are they talking about someone who scaled fast, or someone who built culture?
- Are they talking about execution or influence?
- What's valued here?

---

## About Critical Harm

### "What does 'critical harm' mean in Roblox's strategy? Is it primarily child safety, or does it include creator protections, financial fraud, etc.?"
Why: "Critical harm" can mean different things. You want to be aligned.

**Listen for:**
- Are they focused on protecting kids or protecting the platform?
- Is this a compliance-driven initiative or genuinely about user safety?
- How much ambiguity is there in what counts as "critical harm"?

---

### "How mature is your ML capability in critical harm detection? Are you currently using rule-based systems, simple ML models, or more sophisticated approaches?"
Why: Understanding their current tech tells you what you're building on and where the gaps are.

**Listen for:**
- Are they advanced or early-stage?
- Do they have data infrastructure or are you building it?
- What's the bottleneck: data, compute, expertise, or something else?

---

### "What's the regulatory landscape you're operating in? (COPPA, Online Safety Bill, etc.)"
Why: Regulations heavily shape critical harm strategy.

**Listen for:**
- Are they ahead of regulation or behind?
- Is there legal/policy expertise to lean on?
- Are regulations driving product decisions?

---

### "How do you measure success in critical harm? Is it % of harms caught, false positive rate, user feedback, regulatory compliance, or something else?"
Why: Metrics tell you what the org actually cares about.

**Listen for:**
- Are they metrics-driven or more qualitative?
- Is there a clear success bar?
- Do all stakeholders agree on metrics?

---

## About the Organization

### "What's the leadership style and decision-making culture like here?"
Why: You want to know if you'll thrive. Are they collaborative or hierarchical? Data-driven or intuition-based?

**Listen for:**
- How much autonomy do managers have?
- How fast do decisions happen?
- Is there psychological safety to disagree?

---

### "How are technical and product decisions made together? Are there any recent examples of tension between safety and user experience?"
Why: This is YOUR core skill—navigating tradeoffs. You want to know if the org is good at this.

**Listen for:**
- Do they have a framework for tradeoffs or is it ad-hoc?
- Who has veto power?
- Are mistakes owned or blamed?

---

## Questions That Signal You're Serious

### "What do you think I should learn about before my next interview?"
Why: This shows you're eager to learn and gives you homework. It also builds rapport.

---

### "Is there a technical deep dive I should prepare for? What aspects of the work would I be discussing with the team?"
Why: You want to know what to prep for and what they care about technically.

---

### "What does a 100-day plan look like for someone in this role?"
Why: Shows you think about the first phase strategically.

**Listen for:**
- Do they have a plan or is it open-ended?
- Are they looking for quick wins or long-term building?

---

## Questions NOT to Ask (Save for later)

- ❌ "What's the compensation?" (Too early—comes from HR or later rounds)
- ❌ "What are the problems with the current team?" (Don't invite gossip)
- ❌ "Why did the last person leave?" (Same—too negative)
- ❌ "How much vacation?" (Can ask, but not the vibe-setter)

---

## How to Ask Questions

**Do:**
- Ask with genuine curiosity, not cynicism
- Follow up: "That's interesting, tell me more about..."
- Take notes (signals you care)
- Connect answers to what you'll do: "So if tradeoff-making is important, I'd probably..."

**Don't:**
- Ask as if testing them ("Well actually...")
- Ask the same question to multiple people (shows you weren't listening before)
- Ask hypotheticals without context ("What would you do if...?")
- Dominate the conversation with your own stories (balance)

---

## The Golden Question

If you get one last chance to ask something, ask this:

**"Is there anything about my background or this conversation that makes you concerned about whether I'm the right fit for this role? I'd rather address it now than have it come up later."**

Why: It signals confidence, gives you a chance to course-correct, and shows you care about being a good fit.
