Hey, I'm Jiting. I'm currently a tech lead and manager at DoorDash in our opex org, and I'm leading the ML and AI team. We've got about 12 engineers split across a couple of pods—predictive ML, agentic AI, and our risk data platform. We're focused on three main areas: customer support automation, fraud detection, and abuse prevention. It's high-impact work because everything we do directly affects platform trust and user safety.

What's been really exciting is building and leading this team from scratch. When I started, we had nothing. My role covers a lot—I'm thinking about the long-term technical vision, the planning and roadmaps, hitting milestones, training and growing the junior engineers, handling cross-functional alignment with product and business, and then also the hiring and coaching.

So it's really about making sure we have the right people, they're growing, we're hitting our milestones, and we're executing on a solid technical vision. My day-to-day is a mix of things. I'm doing the typical manager stuff—long-term strategy, roadmapping, making sure we're aligned with our cross-functional partners. But I also stay really hands-on technically. I review major technical decisions, I participate in code reviews on complex ML systems, I investigate tricky production issues alongside the team. I think that technical credibility is crucial when you're leading engineers—they need to trust that you actually understand the work.

When I'm training and developing junior engineers, I'm not just focusing on technical skills. I also help them develop people skills and gain product and project management experience—so they're growing as well-rounded engineers and potential future leaders. That's how you build a strong organization.

Before DoorDash, I worked at VoltronData and claypot as an engineering manager, where I led development of open source projects. I worked on Ibis—which is kind of like pandas, but scalable. The cool thing about it is that you write your analytical code once in Python, and then you can execute it across more than 20 different query engines—DuckDB, BigQuery, Spark, Snowflake, etc. So instead of being limited to what fits in memory on your laptop, you can actually run it on big datasets across clusters. I also led development on Ibis-ML, which is similar to scikit-learn for preprocessing and feature engineering, but again, scalable. So you get the same API but you're not limited by memory constraints.

Before that, I spent time at Airbnb as a tech lead. I built the location graph—everything from data collection to ranking and understanding. This was a fascinating project about location data: how we collect it, ingest it, store it at scale, rank it, and help people understand it. Our system powers a lot of features on the Airbnb platform—unique listings search, the map, autocomplete. It was a core piece of the platform, so we had to get it right.

I also worked on the user engagement team at Airbnb. That's where I really got excited about the intersection of data and impact. We were figuring out how to send the right content to the right users at the right time to drive engagement and conversion. That's where I learned a lot about thinking end-to-end about impact, not just building systems in isolation.

Before Airbnb, I was a machine learning engineer at eBay and then at Advanced America. Those were earlier-stage roles where I was really just building ML models and features.

So, I'd say my arc has been: IC engineer → ML specialist → tech lead → manager. And what I've learned through all of that is that it's not about picking a lane. The best teams are led by people who still understand the technical work deeply, who can make good judgment calls, and who care about developing people. That's what drives me.
