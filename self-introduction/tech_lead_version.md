# Self-Introduction: Tech Lead Version

**Emphasize: Technical depth, systems thinking, architectural decisions, technical leadership**

---

I'm Jiting, and I'm the tech lead manager at DoorDash in our opex org, leading the ML and AI team. We're building machine learning and agentic AI systems to solve fraud detection and customer support automation at scale. I've got about 12 engineers split across a couple of pods — predictive ML, agentic AI, and platform.

I focus on two main things: the technical side and the people side. 

On the technical side, I'm thinking about the long-term vision, the architecture, and making sure we're building the right systems at the right scale. That means planning, roadmaps, hitting milestones, reviewing design documents, participating in code reviews on complex ML systems, and also doing the implementation myself—writing code, staying hands-on with execution. I help the team navigate technical tradeoffs.

On the people and leadership side, I'm training and growing junior engineers. 
 - I want them to think end-to-end about systems, not just write code. Through code reviews and daily work, I help them understand the bigger picture and elevate their technical judgment.
 - I also help them develop people skills and get product and project management experience—so they're growing as well-rounded engineers and potential future leaders. 
 
 I'm also bridging with cross-functional teams — product, business, data — to understand business needs and make sure our technical decisions actually serve what the clients needs. 
 - That means making the right design decisions, communicating clearly across teams, and unblocking people so they can do their best work. It's translating between what's technically possible and what the business needs, then building a roadmap that works for both.

Before DoorDash, I worked at VoltronData and claypot on open source projects—specifically Ibis and Ibis-ML. Ibis is a really interesting technical challenge. Think of it like pandas, but scalable—you write your code once and it compiles to 20+ different query engines. 

With Ibis-ML, we extended IBIS to feature engineering and preprocessing workflows—similar to scikit-learn preprocessing, but instead of being limited to in-memory datasets, you can run it on big clusters.

At Airbnb, I was the tech lead and built the location graph—everything from data collection to location ranking and understanding. That was a large-scale data system handling millions of location data points. We had to think carefully about data collection, storage, indexing, ranking, and serving. The interesting part was that location data is fundamental to Airbnb—it powers search, the map, autocomplete, recommendations.
 - So we were constantly making tradeoffs between latency, accuracy, freshness, and cost. And we had to make those decisions in a way that served multiple teams downstream.

I also worked on user engagement systems at Airbnb—figuring out how to send the right content to the right users at the right time.

Earlier in my career, I was an ML engineer at eBay and Advanced America, building and deploying ML models for fraud detection.

What I think makes me effective as a tech lead is that I care about understanding systems end-to-end.
 >  It's not enough to build something that works in a vacuum—you have to think about how it operates in production, how it scales, how it interacts with other systems, and how it serves the business. 
 I really enjoy mentoring other engineers to think that way too. I invest in growing junior engineers' technical judgment.

And I make sure we're always aligned with cross-functional partners—product, business, data teams—so that our technical vision actually drives platform impact.

 I think good technical leadership is about elevating the team's technical judgment, developing the people around you, and making sure your technical decisions are grounded in business reality and user impact.
